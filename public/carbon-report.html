Executive Summary

Carbon accounting is now a regulated, growing enterprise need

This report responds to a directive to research the carbon-footprinting domain and propose a plan for a dedicated Carbon Footprint application — separate from the SystemETA ERP — replacing the small module currently embedded in the platform.

1

Carbon accounting is standardised globally through the GHG Protocol, which classifies emissions into Scope 1, 2, and 3 — the accepted foundation for any serious enterprise product.

2

The core calculation is simple — Activity Data × Emission Factor = Emissions — but the operational complexity lies in emission-factor libraries, boundary-setting, and Scope 3 supply-chain data.

3

SEBI's BRSR / BRSR Core already mandates GHG disclosure for the top 1,000 listed Indian companies, with Scope 3 phasing in from FY 2025-26. Demand is real and growing.

4

Market leaders (SAP SFM, Persefoni, Climatiq, Plan A) show the winning wedge is now AI — automated factor mapping, OCR ingestion, anomaly detection, and scenario modelling.

5

A standalone architecture is justified: it scales independently, sells beyond ERP customers, and keeps pace with evolving disclosure standards.

Recommendation

Build in three phases — each shipping something demonstrable before the next begins.

1 · Understanding Carbon Footprint

The Scope 1 / 2 / 3 backbone

A carbon footprint is the total greenhouse gases released — directly or indirectly — by an activity, product, organisation, or individual, expressed in kg CO₂e. The GHG Protocol splits an organisation's emissions into three scopes; every serious carbon product is built around this classification.

ScopeCoversTypical sources
Scope 1 — DirectEmissions from sources owned or directly controlled by the company.Company vehicles, on-site fuel combustion, furnaces, refrigerant leaks, backup generators.
Scope 2 — EnergyEmissions from purchased electricity, heat, or steam.Grid electricity for offices, servers, plants; purchased district heating/cooling.
Scope 3 — Value chainAll other indirect emissions, upstream and downstream. Voluntary today, becoming mandatory.Purchased goods, business travel, employee commute, distribution, use of sold products, waste.

Why Scope 3 matters: for most companies it accounts for roughly 90% of total emissions — and it's the hardest to measure, since it requires data from suppliers, employees, and downstream users.

The universal calculation

Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Activity Data × Emission Factor
Diesel burnt in a generator 100 L × 2.68 kg CO₂e/L = 268 kg CO₂e
Electricity, Indian grid 10,000 kWh × 0.716 (CEA) = 7,160 kg CO₂e
Domestic flight, economy 1,200 km × 0.156 kg CO₂e/pkm = 187 kg CO₂e

Emission factor databases

  • DEFRA (UK) — widely used, free, published annually.
  • US EPA / EIA — detailed North American factors.
  • IPCC — scientific baseline for national inventories.
  • CEA (India) — official grid emission factors.
  • Ecoinvent, GLEC, EXIOBASE, Climatiq — commercial life-cycle databases.

Regulatory landscape

  • SEBI BRSR — mandatory since FY 2022-23 for the top 1,000 listed companies.
  • BRSR Core — phased third-party assurance, top 150 → top 1,000 by FY 2026-27.
  • Scope 3 — top 250 companies, comply-or-explain from FY 2025-26.
  • CCTS — compliance carbon market live for obligated entities from FY26.
  • EU CSRD / CBAM — relevant for Indian exporters to Europe.
2 · Current Module in SystemETA

Why the embedded module doesn't scale

SystemETA currently includes a small carbon-footprinting module embedded in the ERP. Based on observation, it supports manual entry for a small number of sources against a flat, largely hard-coded factor table, with a basic total-CO₂e dashboard — no scope classification, no regulator-recognised export, no Scope 3, no supplier data. To be confirmed with the module's current owner.

LimitationWhy it blocks growth
Scope not modelledCannot satisfy BRSR or any global disclosure framework — all are scope-based.
No emission-factor libraryEvery calculation is only as accurate as one static table; no versioning, no region fallbacks.
Coupled to ERP customers onlyCan't be sold to companies on a different ERP — or none — cutting off most of the market.
No Scope 3 or supplier onboardingValue-chain emissions (~90% of total) stay invisible.
No AI/automationEvery entry is manual; no OCR, anomaly detection, or forecasting.
No regulator-ready reportsBRSR / GHG Protocol / CDP exports aren't generated.
No audit trail or evidence storeFails assurance requirements now applied to BRSR Core filings.

What to carry forward

Existing customer emission data — migratable schema SystemETA design language — dark glassmorphic UI Existing auth — single sign-on for ERP users
3 · Proposed Standalone Application

Eight core modules

A separate application scales independently, sells to non-ERP customers, ships on its own cadence as regulation evolves, and keeps a purpose-built audit trail for assurance.

01

Organisation & Boundary Setup

Reporting entity, facilities, business units, reporting period, base year, consolidation approach.

02

Data Collection & Ingestion

Manual forms, CSV/Excel upload, ERP connector, utility-bill OCR, IoT/meter feeds, supplier questionnaires.

03

Emission Factors Library

Versioned, searchable factors from DEFRA, EPA, IPCC, CEA India, plus internal factors with region/year fallback.

04

Calculation Engine

Applies the correct factor per activity, handles unit conversions, tracks assumptions for audit.

05

Dashboards & Analytics

Real-time footprint by scope, category, site, product; trend, hotspot, and intensity analysis.

06

Reporting & Compliance

One-click exports for BRSR, BRSR Core, GHG Protocol, CDP, GRI, CSRD, with evidence pack attached.

07

Reduction Planning & Targets

Absolute/intensity/SBTi targets, reduction scenarios, initiative tracking, net-zero forecasting.

08

User, Roles & Audit Log

Role-based access, full change history, approval workflows for period close.

Calculations the engine must support

Scope 1 — stationary & mobile combustion Fuel × EF (diesel, petrol, LPG, CNG, natural gas, coal)
Scope 1 — refrigerant leakage kg lost × GWP (e.g. R-410A = 2,088)
Scope 2 — purchased electricity location-based & market-based, side by side per GHG Protocol guidance
Scope 3 — value chain (15 categories) 3 launch categories: purchased goods, business travel, employee commute
Product Carbon Footprint ISO 14067, later phase

3.4 · AI & ML Features

Where modern carbon platforms differentiate

Six capabilities, ranked by value delivered per unit of effort.

01

Emission-Factor Mapping Assistant

Given "airfreight Mumbai → Frankfurt, 200 kg," suggests the top three matching factors with confidence scores.

Cuts data-entry time ~70% — the biggest single win
02

OCR + LLM Invoice Ingestion

Upload utility bills, fuel receipts, freight invoices; the model extracts date, quantity, unit, supplier.

Removes manual typing; feeds the evidence store
03

Anomaly Detection

Flags entries that deviate from historical patterns before they enter the final report.

Prevents embarrassing errors, speeds audits
04

Predictive Forecasting

Time-series models forecast next quarter's emissions from planned activity and seasonality.

Turns the tool into a planning system
05

Scenario Simulator

"What if we switch 40% of grid electricity to solar?" — models the delta on total emissions and targets.

Drives reduction decisions; a demo favourite
06

Natural-Language Assistant (RAG)

Chat grounded in the company's own emissions data plus GHG Protocol / BRSR knowledge.

Executive-friendly; strong in sales demos
